What we do today

The everyday help that lets someone stay in their own home, with their own routine — provided today, no waiting.

Companionship

Someone to sit with over a pot of tea, share the news of the day, or simply not be alone in the house. Often the reason a family gets in touch first.

Help at home

Light cleaning, tidying, the laundry and the ironing — the small jobs that quietly get harder, kept on top of so the house stays comfortable.

Shopping & prescriptions

The weekly shop, a few things picked up, and prescriptions collected from the pharmacy — so nobody has to go without or make the trip in bad weather.

Simple meal preparation

A hot meal made with the toaster, microwave and air fryer. No oven, no hob, no flame — we tell you that plainly, because it's the honest scope of what we do.

Accompanied outings

A steady arm to the appointment, the shops, church, or just a walk in the fresh air. Getting out of the house is often the thing that lifts the week.

Respite sitting

Companionship-based sitting so a family carer can keep their own appointment, sleep, or simply leave the house — and come back to someone rested.

A day at home

Light changing through the house

What a day with us can look like — quiet, unhurried, shaped around the person, not the clock.

Morning

Curtains and the kettle

Curtains open, the kettle on, breakfast and the first tablets of the day set out. A calm start beats a rushed one.

Late morning

Out and about

A walk to the shop, a lift to the appointment, or the washing hung out while there's a bit of sun. Company for the errands nobody likes doing alone.

Afternoon

Settled and tidy

The ironing done, a warm meal from the air fryer, and time to sit and talk. This is often the part of the visit that matters most.

Evening

Lamps on, doors locked

Supper, the lamps on, doors checked and locked. We let the family know how the day went before we leave.

For everyone at the table

Working for the whole family

When someone starts needing help at home, more than one life changes. We treat the family as the people we work for — not an audience to be updated afterwards.

You hear from us after every visit

What we did, how they seemed, anything worth knowing. You shouldn't have to ring to find out how your mother's day went.

One number, one person

The same familiar voice when you call. Not a call centre, not a ticket, not a different name each time.

We say what we notice

Early, while it's still a small thing. Honest observations are worth more than reassurance.

Respite is for the family carer

Where we stand with the CQC

Arkand Care Ltd is registered at Companies House in England and Wales (no. 17291178). We are not registered with the Care Quality Commission, and we do not provide personal care.

Companionship, help around the home, shopping, simple meal preparation and accompanied outings are not regulated activities, and we provide them today. Washing, dressing and medication are regulated, and we will not offer them until we are registered to.

We would rather tell you what we cannot do than be vague about what we can.

Coming soon — subject to CQC registration

Personal care, dementia support, medication support, night care and support coming home from hospital are regulated services. We will offer them once the Care Quality Commission has registered us, and not before.

If you'd like us to let you know when that happens, say so and we will.

  • Personal care (washing, dressing)
  • Dementia support
  • Medication support
  • Night & overnight care
  • Hospital discharge support
