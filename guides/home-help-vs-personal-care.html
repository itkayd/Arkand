Home help vs personal care in England

Two kinds of support that are easy to confuse — and one important line between them, drawn by regulation.

By Arkand Care · Published 22 August 2026 · Reviewed 22 August 2026

When you start looking for help at home, you meet two phrases quickly: home help and personal care. They sound similar, and some providers offer both, but in England they are treated very differently — and knowing which one you need makes choosing simpler and safer.

What counts as home help

Home help (sometimes called domestic help or practical support) covers the everyday tasks that keep a household ticking over. Typically that means:

  • Light housework, laundry and tidying.
  • Shopping, collecting prescriptions and running errands.
  • Preparing simple meals and a hot drink.
  • Company, and help getting to appointments or out and about.

This is support around a person rather than on them. On its own, it does not usually involve touching or intimate assistance.

What counts as personal care

Personal care is hands-on help with the body and with health tasks. The NHS describes home care (domiciliary care) as including help with things like washing, dressing and taking medicines.1 In practice, personal care usually means:

  • Help with washing, bathing and using the toilet.
  • Help with dressing and continence.
  • Assistance with eating.
  • Support with medicines.

The line that matters: CQC registration

Here is the key difference. In England, providing personal care to people in their own homes is a regulated activity. Any organisation that does it must be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), the independent regulator of health and social care, and is inspected against national standards.2 The CQC’s scope of registration sets out that personal care — help with washing, dressing and similar — falls within regulation.3

Home help and companionship, provided on their own, generally fall outside that requirement. That does not make them lesser — it simply means the safeguards you should look for are different. With a registered personal-care provider, you can read a CQC inspection report. With a home-help provider, you should look instead at how they recruit, how they handle concerns, and whether the same reliable person visits.

Why the distinction is useful to you

  • It tells you what to check. If you need personal care, confirm the provider is CQC-registered. If you need home help, ask about dependability, recruitment checks and complaints handling.
  • It affects cost and funding. Your local council may assess needs and, depending on circumstances, help with arranging or funding care.4
  • It can change over time. Many people begin with home help and companionship, then add personal care later as needs grow. The two can run side by side.
